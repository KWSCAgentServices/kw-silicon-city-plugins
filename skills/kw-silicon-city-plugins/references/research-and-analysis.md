# Property, market, and neighborhood research

## Research defaults

Use the supplied property, client criteria, geography, and timeframe. If no period is supplied, use the latest available month or quarter and label it; compare the same periods and property types. A city-level request can use city boundaries as a stated default. Ask when an unspecified location or subject makes useful research impossible.

Retrieve current primary listing/brokerage records, public records, local market reports, and authoritative public sources. Record retrieval dates and data periods. Distinguish live asking price, closed sale price, estimated value, pending status, and historical records. Corroborate stale or conflicting publication-critical facts using the existing source workflow. Never invent comps or inaccessible MLS fields.

## Property analysis and comparisons

Create a compact fact table: address, status, asking/sale price, beds, baths, Approx. living area, Approx. lot area where expressed in square feet, property type, available HOA costs, verified features, and source/date. Omit unavailable facts or mark them unknown. Separate observed condition from listing claims and inspection findings. Flag permits, zoning, insurance, HOA, condition, and other unanswered questions only when relevant to the subject.

For comparisons, keep units and definitions consistent. Explain tradeoffs against client-stated needs. Label derived calculations and assumptions. Price per square foot uses the verified price and Approx. living area; do not compare lot area with living area. Avoid presenting listing descriptions as independently verified inspection results.

## Comparable analysis

Choose comparable sales with disclosed criteria: proximity, property type, recency, size, age, condition, and relevant features. Prefer closed sales for value evidence and separate active/pending competition. Explain exclusions and any expansion of the search when the local sample is limited. Use justified adjustments with stated assumptions; do not manufacture a standard adjustment amount or suggest precision the evidence cannot support.

Deliver a comp table, selection rationale, a supported range if feasible, and limitations. This is agent decision support, not a certified appraisal. If evidence is insufficient, provide the evidence available and the missing inputs rather than a guessed valuation.

## Reports

Lead with the important finding, then concise metrics and implications. Define geography, property type, period, sample size when known, and metric definitions. Distinguish median from average, list price from sold price, and month-over-month from year-over-year. Do not average incompatible market reports. Cite supporting source links near findings and distinguish interpretation from reported facts.

## Neighborhood research

Use objective location facts and the client's stated priorities: transportation, parks, public amenities, commute assumptions, official school-boundary resources, and relevant public information. Do not infer residents' protected characteristics, steer clients by demographic composition, or label areas as suited to particular protected groups. Provide official resources for school assignment and safety questions and avoid unsupported quality or safety claims.

## Review

Check facts and calculations, consistent comparison definitions, current status, measurement qualifiers, and source dates before delivery. For legal, financial, tax, lending, zoning, or insurance guidance, verify current authoritative sources and distinguish informational findings from professional determinations. Do not claim guaranteed returns, financing eligibility, or future appreciation.
