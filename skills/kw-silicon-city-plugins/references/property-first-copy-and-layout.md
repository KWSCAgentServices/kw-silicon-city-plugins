# Property-first copy and layout

Use this structure for every flyer, social caption, email, postcard, brochure panel, carousel card, and other marketing piece.

Apply [plain-writing.md](plain-writing.md) when drafting and reviewing all copy, including headline, CTA, caption and text supplied to GPT Image. Keep the required short feature bullets and milestone messages; write them without stock slogans, inflated claims or repeated rhetorical patterns.

## Core principle

The property is the hero. Lead with what is valuable, distinctive, or useful to the prospective buyer. Agent identity, portrait, and contact information support the property presentation; they must never dominate it.

Use a helpful, confident, factual tone. Do not sound pushy, self-promotional, or overly sales-driven. Avoid unsupported urgency, hype, generic praise, and agent-centered openings.

## Opportunity-led communication

Default to opportunity-led, value-first communication across listing copy, flyers, social posts, email subjects and bodies, campaign pieces, invitations and promotional follow-ups. The reader should quickly understand "What can this help me do or decide?" rather than only "What happened?" Deliver useful value in the message itself before asking the reader to contact the agent. Do not gate every useful detail behind a lead-capture invitation.

Translate a verified feature or update into a modest, concrete reader benefit: a kitchen island can offer additional preparation space; a verified patio offers an outdoor space to explore; an open house offers a chance to assess the layout in person. Keep the distinction between the verified fact and a possible use clear. Do not infer permitted additions, subdivision, rental income, affordability, financing eligibility, investment returns, market bargains or future appreciation from lot size, price or appearance alone.

Use this communication arc within the existing short, scannable copy hierarchy:

1. Attention through relevance: lead with a specific opportunity or benefit that matters to the intended reader. Retain mandatory JUST LISTED, OPEN HOUSE or truthful closing/status messaging; immediately pair it with the reader-facing value. A generic "New listing" or "Market update" alone is not a sufficient hook.
2. Value before promotion: provide three to five concise, verified details explaining what is worth exploring or understanding. Connect features to everyday use without fabricating improvements or overstating suitability. Keep essentials and applicable attribution visible.
3. A useful next step: offer one relevant action, such as viewing the expanded gallery, exploring the home in person, or reviewing comparable properties. State what the reader will receive or be able to assess, not merely "Call me" or "Don't miss out." Keep the request low-pressure and the agent/contact block secondary.

"Opportunity" describes the framing, not a required keyword or a claim that every home is a bargain. Avoid repetitive "amazing opportunity" phrasing, FOMO, invented deadlines, guaranteed results, or unsupported "rare", "exclusive" and "below market" claims. Do not assert that this framing increases conversions without measured evidence. Remain inclusive; do not target or exclude people using protected characteristics.

Examples below illustrate framing only, not current listing facts or ready-to-publish claims:

- With a verified island and pantry: replace "New listing with a kitchen island and pantry" with "Just Listed | Extra prep space and pantry storage." Follow with the actual verified details, without adding a renovation claim.
- With a verified open-house event: replace "Open house this weekend" with "Open House | Walk through the layout and see how the spaces connect." Include the verified event details and a useful invitation; do not invent an event date.
- With sourced market data: replace "Here is the latest market news" with "Considering a move? See what recent comparable sales can help you evaluate." Include the actual dated findings and limitations; do not promise a price or treat an entire market as a bargain.
- For a verified closing: keep the truthful closing result and actual agent role, then offer a practical takeaway or comparison relevant to nearby owners. Do not turn a sold home into an available buying opportunity or imply the result is guaranteed for another seller.

Before release, check that the benefit has supporting evidence, useful detail is already present, the next step helps the reader, and the agent is not the main story. Apply status-specific price/date omissions, Approx. measurement qualifiers, roster privacy, original-photo/face rules and destination requirements unchanged. Do not force promotional opportunity language into contract summaries, safety notices, transaction deadlines, neutral research or routine operational replies.

## Purpose-led flyer guide

Determine the flyer's communication job from the request and verified status before choosing headline, hero photo, facts and action. These purpose rules override generic hierarchy defaults, but never override truth, attribution, identity or status-specific price/date safeguards.

- Just Listed / active-property interest: create interest in the specific home. Lead with its strongest original photo and a verified benefit for daily life, not a bedroom-count headline. For a verified new launch, retain the prominent JUST LISTED milestone message plus the benefit-led subheadline. An established active listing must not be relabeled newly listed.
- Open House: invite attendance. Make OPEN HOUSE, the verified date/time, address and focused invitation immediately findable. Do not invent an event or hide its details under agent branding.
- Just Sold / Just Closed: build neighborhood credibility through a verified completed-sale result and the promoting agent's name with their truthful role. Lead copy with the verified result and name; keep the property photograph dominant and portrait compact. Confirm the agent's actual participation before implying they sold, listed or represented this transaction. Preserve co-listing attribution. Never substitute an asking price for a closed sale price or invent above-asking, speed, record-sale or neighborhood-ranking claims. Omit dates unless requested, consistent with the milestone rule.

### Seven design decisions

1. Choose one clear hero photograph. Prefer available professional original photography. Judge exterior, kitchen, main living space and other views for appeal and relevance; do not automatically choose the first exterior. A compelling bright kitchen or interior may lead. Supporting photos reveal other useful spaces without competing equally with the hero.
2. Use a concise benefit-led headline tied to verified features and everyday living. For an event or completed sale, lead with that truthful purpose and use a relevant benefit/result subheadline. Do not merely repeat specifications.
3. Keep address and permitted price visible but secondary to the purpose/benefit. In Contract remains price-free. Sold price means a verified closed amount, not the former asking price.
4. Use three to five short bullets. Prioritize differentiators, then concise essentials; do not invent uniqueness when only basic facts are available. Keep every sq. ft. value individually qualified with Approx.
5. Give readers one clear action and one verified phone number. Default to a focused invitation suited to the person's role, such as requesting details or a tour referral for staff. Keep the phone slightly larger than supporting contact text. Include the matched person's verified email as a secondary contact line under the approved warm editorial standard; honor a no-email request or destination restriction. Omit multiple phones, social handles and competing website actions unless requested or required; never delete required identification or attribution.
6. A QR code must add useful property-specific value: an accessible video tour, expanded photo gallery or verified showing scheduler. Verify the exact destination and its function before design; describe what the scan provides. Do not use a generic homepage or decorative/fake QR. Supply a correctly encoded QR asset when host capabilities permit and decode/scan the finished design at delivery size to prove it resolves to the exact approved destination. It must support the same focused buyer journey, not create competing invitations. When no useful destination or reliable scan verification is available, omit the code and explain that omission briefly; never insert it later with HTML/code to bypass the whole-GPT-Image workflow.
7. Remove visual clutter: no clip art, decorative feature icons, gradients, dated novelty fonts or unnecessary contact channels. Use readable contemporary typography, controlled color and whitespace. Rectangular photo crops are allowed; avoid rigid disconnected boxes and awkward imbalance, not rectangles themselves. Connect elements through shared alignments, consistent spacing and intentional visual weight.

### Three-question release test

At a quick scan, can a reader identify (1) the appeal or purpose, (2) the essential facts, and (3) the next step? If any answer is unclear, cut distracting decoration or competing messages, strengthen hierarchy, and review again. This check supplements, never replaces, unchanged-face/photo/logo, factual, attribution and channel checks. Do not claim conversion performance without actual evidence.

## Warm home-first design direction

### Approved warm editorial standard

The user's approved "Come Home to Gilroy" reference establishes the default visual language for residential marketing: warm ivory/cream, KW red and charcoal, elegant contemporary serif headlines, restrained handwritten photo captions, generous real-home photography and a welcoming feeling of everyday living. Reuse this style, not that property's facts, agent identity, generated photos or portrait. Adapt the hero image and hierarchy to the purpose-led guide; a strong interior may still lead. A later user-specified style or approved template can override the default.

- Flyer color preference, October 30, 2026: use black or near-black charcoal for the headline and most other text, including the permitted price, event details, feature bullets, phone and email. Keep KW red #CE011F to small optional accents such as a fine rule or a short understated detail; it need not appear outside the original logo. Avoid large red headlines, red text blocks, red banners and oversized red price badges by default. This replaces the earlier red-headline preference for all flyers, including flyers within campaigns. Use typography, spacing and property photography to create an elegant, professional hierarchy without making the design generic. Other formats follow their specific brief; honor a later explicit color request.
- Keep the warm ivory/cream or white canvas. Warm ivory around #F8F3E8 is a working design recommendation, not an official KW color. Black text does not mean a black background: preserve the complete logo's original colors on white and keep the portrait and property photos unchanged. For text over a photo, choose a readable placement or a small contrasting white text treatment when necessary; never sacrifice contrast or alter the original photo to force black text.
- Use elegant readable serif display type with clean supporting typography, not dated novelty fonts. Small handwritten captions may express a verified photographed space, such as "Room to gather" for a kitchen; avoid long script text, poor contrast or obstruction of features.
- Soft paper texture and one restrained warm underline or subtle hand-painted natural edge accent are permitted as part of this approved style. Keep accents in graphic margins, outside all property photography and portraits. They are not permission for stock clip art, icon badges, gradients, excessive foliage, invented landscaping or decorative clutter.
- Make one genuine property photo the hero with substantial complementary views. Connect all elements through consistent spacing, shared alignments, repeated type/color treatment and balanced visual weight. Avoid disconnected rigid boxes, awkward asymmetry, oversized price badges, bulky contact strips or a corporate black-and-gold default. Rectangular photo crops are allowed; creative cohesion does not require warping or blending rooms.
- Keep portrait, agent name and contact smaller but readable. Use one verified phone as the primary action and include that same person's verified email as a secondary contact line by default when available. The email is not a competing second CTA. Omit extra websites, social handles and phones unless requested or required. Honor an explicit no-email request or destination restriction; never invent an email or reuse another agent's.
- Phone emphasis stays modest: roughly 10–20% larger than nearby email/role details, not an oversized page headline. Keep full logo artwork unchanged on a naturally integrated plain white area, never black.
- Do not add "Design Review", "Not for Distribution" or similar review labels to the artwork unless requested. Report unresolved readiness limits clearly in the chat/handoff instead; removing a label never passes the unchanged-face/photo/logo or other release gates.


The user prefers an eye-catching, welcoming editorial design that helps buyers feel the home before deciding to visit. Use that as the default for residential listing marketing unless an approved template or user brief directs otherwise. For first-time buyers, make the home easy to understand without implying affordability or buyer eligibility that has not been verified.

Retrieve and inspect the supplied MLS gallery beyond the first exterior photo. Select complementary original views that tell a useful home story: arrival/exterior, cooking/kitchen, relaxing/living area and, when available, an actual outdoor space or other distinctive room. Prefer one strong hero plus two or more useful supporting views when the format and host reference limit permit; do not flood the model with duplicates. If references are limited, retain the original portrait and complete logo, and distribute additional verified rooms across coordinated pieces rather than silently omitting mandatory assets.

Create warmth with welcoming, property-grounded headlines, generous photography, readable editorial typography, soft warm surrounding neutrals and restrained KW red accents. Keep the complete logo unchanged. Evocative captions such as "Room to gather" or "Space to unwind" must match the photographed space. Do not alter the actual photos' lighting, scenery or rooms; add furniture, people, family scenes or decor; or use virtually staged photos as real property photography. Convey everyday living and belonging inclusively, without preferences about familial status or other protected characteristics.

The home must remain dominant; keep the original, unchanged agent headshot small but assessable and the supporting contact block compact. The phone is slightly larger within that block, not the page's hero. Keep short feature bullets, the low-pressure CTA and readable attribution. Warmth never overrides face, property, fact or logo fidelity. Do not copy a flawed generated example literally.

### Monochrome editorial flyer reference

The November 5, 2026 open-house sample is a style reference, not a listing source or fixed template. Use this direction by default for property flyers, including campaign flyers. ## Status-specific price and date rules

Apply these rules before drafting copy, assembling GPT Image display copy and reviewing every standalone asset or campaign piece:

- In Contract: when the verified listing status is "In Contract", omit all property price information from public marketing, including list/asking price, contract price, sale price, price ranges and price badges. Apply the omission to visuals, captions, email, flyers, carousels and stories, not just the headline. Use the status and property value/features instead. Apply the same omission to a source-verified equivalent under-contract status, without guessing a status mapping. Do not expose a non-public accepted-offer amount.
- Just Listed and Just Closed: omit calendar dates by default from the entire announcement, including graphics, captions, body, footer and reusable campaign adaptations. Include dates only when the agent explicitly asks to display them; a date found in the source is not a request. Do not ask whether to include a date when the default is sufficient. Verify listing/closing dates internally to establish the truthful milestone, but keep them out of final marketing copy and the image model's display fields.
- Verify "Just Closed" against a completed closing; being In Contract, pending or having an estimated closing date does not establish a closed sale. Verify "Just Listed" against listing history as already required.
- These display rules override generic instructions to show price or dates. They do not remove internal source/retrieval dates, dates in private research or transaction documents, or verified event dates from a separately requested open-house invitation. If an agent expressly requests an open-house announcement together with a Just Listed message, the requested event date/time may appear, but do not add listing dates.
- Before generation, separate internal verification data from approved display fields. Remove suppressed amounts and dates from display copy and explicitly instruct GPT Image "Do not display any price" for In Contract and "Do not display any calendar date" for date-free milestone pieces. Never ask the model to choose which raw source fields to publish.
- Reject output that leaks an omitted price/date into a badge, caption or disclosure, or restores it during adaptation or correction. Correct image defects through GPT Image and copy defects before delivery.

## Mandatory copy hierarchy

Every standalone piece begins with a short, attention-grabbing headline. The headline must be grounded in verified property value or event context, not clickbait. Favor a meaningful differentiator such as usable land, indoor-outdoor living, a verified renovation, a view, flexible space, a location benefit, or the open-house opportunity. If no distinctive feature is verified, use a clear event-led or property-type headline rather than inventing one.

After the headline, use this order:

1. Optional one-sentence hook that explains the buyer value in plain language.
2. Address and permitted price or requested event details, kept compact; apply the status-specific price and date rules above first.
3. Three to five short bullets containing the most important verified facts and differentiators. For a property flyer with an HOA, include the approved fee/frequency line in the compact property facts/costs area or as one of these bullets, for example "HOA: $350/month". Do not bury it in fine print, omit it to make room for promotional copy, or add unnecessary bullets. Follow the HOA verification procedure in source-workflow.md; the example amount is not a listing fact.
4. A low-pressure call to action focused on helping the buyer, such as scheduling a private tour or getting additional property details.
5. A compact supporting agent/contact block.
6. Required listing-agent attribution and disclosures in a readable footer.

Do not open with a dense sentence containing the address, status, price, beds, baths, measurements, contact details, listing attribution, and MLS number. Do not turn the body into a paragraph when the same information can be scanned as bullets.

Each bullet should communicate one idea or one tightly related group of facts. Put the most decision-relevant or differentiating feature first. Basic specifications may be combined compactly, for example:

- 3 bedrooms • 3 bathrooms
- Approx. 2,371 sq. ft. of living space
- Approx. 0.47-acre lot
- [Verified standout feature]

A general headline such as “Beautiful Home for Sale” is too weak when a more specific verified value can be stated. Do not use clichés such as “dream home,” “won't last,” “act fast,” “once-in-a-lifetime,” or “luxury” unless the source and user direction genuinely support them.

## Format-specific application

- Flyer or postcard: place the headline at the top, let property photography carry the page, keep the feature list short, and put agent/contact and attribution information in a smaller lower section.
- Social caption: use the headline as the first line, a brief hook only when useful, three to five bullets, then the CTA and attribution. Avoid an opening paragraph or data dump.
- Email: use an attention-worthy factual subject line, repeat or sharpen the headline in the opening, keep the body brief, and present key details as bullets.
- Carousel or story: every card that may circulate alone needs a clear property-led message. Use the first card for the headline and hero property image; use later cards for photos and one concise value point each. Keep the agent block secondary.
- Brochure or multi-page piece: maintain the property-led hierarchy on every spread. Additional space allows more imagery and detail, not a larger agent promotion.

## Property-first visual hierarchy

Property photography must occupy the largest and most visually prominent areas. When enough authorized listing photos are available and the format supports them, use multiple property images rather than giving unused space to agent branding. Prefer one strong hero image plus supporting photos that reveal different valuable aspects of the home.

Select photos for buyer usefulness: exterior, main living area, kitchen, primary suite, outdoor space, lot, view, or other verified standout features. Do not include near-duplicate images merely to increase the count.

The agent portrait must remain visible and recognizable but secondary:

- place it in a compact contact block;
- keep it substantially smaller than the hero property image;
- do not use it as the background, central focal point, or largest image;
- do not place the agent name above the property headline or at comparable scale;
- keep contact details readable without competing with the property.

The brokerage logo must remain legible but should not overpower the property. Listing attribution and required disclosures may be visually quieter, but they must remain readable at delivery size.

On every visual containing a contact block, set the phone number slightly larger than the surrounding contact text so it is easy to notice and read. Aim for roughly 10–20% larger than the email, website, role, and license text, adjusting to the format. Give it clear contrast and spacing. This emphasis belongs within the compact contact block; the property headline and imagery retain the overall visual hierarchy.

## Review standard

Complete the final editorial pass in plain-writing.md before releasing copy or sending display text to GPT Image. Check the image's rendered wording again before delivery.

Reject and revise any output that:

- begins with a long factual paragraph instead of a headline;
- lacks a property-led, attention-grabbing headline;
- uses more prose than a viewer can quickly scan;
- buries key facts instead of using short bullets;
- makes the agent, portrait, agent name, or brokerage branding more prominent than the property;
- uses too few property photos despite having several useful authorized images and enough layout space;
- sounds pushy, salesy, self-congratulatory, or focused on the agent rather than buyer value;
- merely announces news without a concrete reader benefit, withholds all useful detail until contact, or uses empty opportunity language instead of evidence;
- omits the CTA, attribution, required branding, or measurement qualifiers.
- shows a phone number at the same size as or smaller than the surrounding contact details, or makes it hard to spot.

Correct visual and copy problems through GPT Image when the artifact is an image-generation deliverable. Do not patch the design afterward with HTML or code.
